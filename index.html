Votes, amendments, and sponsorships

Burnett's Voting Record

These are selected consequential votes and affirmative legislative actions, not a complete history. Final-passage votes, amendment votes, and sponsorships are labeled separately. Burnett's clearest affirmative ideological positions appear in failed amendments and bills he sponsored or co-sponsored, which are included below.

Affordability in words. Opposition in the roll call.

Burnett repeatedly presents reduced costs and household affordability as central goals, yet he has voted against enacted rent stabilization, medical-debt credit protections, paid-leave job protections, and a tax on income above a $1 million deduction. Read Burnett's stated priorities.

Showing all selected actions

ESSB 6346 / 2026NO

Voted no to the Millionaires Tax

The enacted law applies a 9.9% tax to Washington taxable income after an inflation-indexed $1 million standard deduction, beginning with tax year 2028. Revenue supports public defense and specified tax relief.

Burnett's stated rationale: He argues the tax is unconstitutional, could later reach lower incomes, and would make the state less affordable for families and small businesses.

Bill and history Burnett's explanation
EHB 1217 / 2025NO

Voted no to Rent Stabilization

The law limits most annual rent increases for existing tenants to 7% plus inflation or 10%, whichever is lower, and generally caps manufactured-home increases at 5%.

The policy dispute: Supporters emphasized predictability and protection from sudden spikes. Opponents argued caps can discourage rental investment and reduce future supply.

Bill and history House roll calls
ESSB 5480 / 2025NO

Voted no to Medical-Debt Credit Protection

The law bars providers and collectors from reporting medical debt to consumer credit agencies. A debt reported in violation of the law becomes void and unenforceable.

Why it reaches beyond a hospital bill: Credit reports can affect access to housing, auto loans, employment, and the interest rates a household pays.

Bill and history Burnett's 2025 roll calls
E2SHB 1213 / 2025NO

Voted no to Paid Family and Medical Leave Protections

The law expanded job protection and continued health-coverage protections for qualifying workers using the state's Paid Family and Medical Leave program, while adding grants for some small employers.

The tradeoff: Workers gain greater job security during serious family or medical events; employers take on broader protection and notice requirements.

Bill and history House roll calls
HB 1215 / 2025NO

Voted no to Patient Control Over Pregnancy Advance Directives

The law removed language from Washington's model living-will form that automatically suspended the directive during pregnancy. It did not require a patient to choose withdrawal of care.

What the vote addressed: Whether pregnancy should automatically override the wishes an adult previously placed in an advance directive.

Bill and history Burnett's 2025 roll calls
ESHB 1531 / 2025NO

Voted no to Science-Based Communicable-Disease Response

The enacted law made it state policy that communicable-disease responses use the best available science and protected the ability of public-health officials to promote evidence-based measures, including vaccination.

Explicit limit: The law stated that it created no new vaccination requirement and granted no new authority to impose one.

Bill and history House roll calls
ESHB 2242 / 2026NO

Voted no to Preventive-Care and Vaccine Access

The law clarified Washington's authority to preserve insurance coverage and public-health access for evidence-based preventive services, including vaccines, as federal recommendations change.

Why it mattered in 2026: The bill was designed to let state standards also consider recommendations from established medical organizations, rather than rely on a single federal body.

Bill and history Burnett's 2026 roll calls
SSB 5917 + 2SSB 6182 / 2026NO

Voted no to Medication-Abortion Access and Patient-Care Grants

Burnett opposed two enacted bills: one broadened distribution of the state's abortion and miscarriage-medication stockpile; the other created grants for direct abortion care funded by assessments on certain health carriers.

Important distinction: One bill concerned an existing medication stockpile. The other created a new grant program and insurer assessment. They are separate policy choices.

ESHB 1531 / 2025

Burnett voted against protecting science-based public-health response.

The enacted law directed state and local communicable-disease responses to use the best available science and protected officials' ability to promote evidence-based measures, including vaccines. It explicitly created no new vaccine requirement and no new power to impose one. Burnett voted no. Enacted law / House roll call

ESHB 2242 / 2026

Burnett voted against preserving optional vaccine coverage and access.

The enacted law authorized Washington's Department of Health to issue evidence-based immunization guidance and maintained insurance and state vaccine-program pathways if federal recommendations changed. It expressly imposed no new immunization requirement. Burnett voted no. Enacted law / Burnett's roll calls

Evidence boundary: This record supports describing a pattern of anti-vaccine politics, particularly opposition to mandates and state vaccine infrastructure. It does not establish that Burnett rejects every vaccine, disputes vaccine efficacy, or opposes routine childhood vaccination.

RCW 42.52.150 / PDC instructions

Accepting the trip can be lawful. Leaving it off the form is a separate question.

State ethics law allows legislators to accept reasonable travel expenses for educational programs sponsored by a government. PDC instructions for the financial-affairs statement nonetheless require reporting the full value of transportation, meals, lodging, and other items provided in connection with such an event.
